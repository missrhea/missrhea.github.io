<a href="https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/195430687-blue-sisters?">Blue Sisters</a> is a moving story about the four March sisters- yes, it was not lost on me that they share the same last name as the sisters from <a href="">Little Women</a>. They were born and raised in New York City, and are the product of a highly dysfunctional family. When Nicky, the most beloved sister, unexpectedly passes away one day, it sends the other three sisters reeling with grief and loss. As the story unfolds and we learn about the three sisters, it becomes clear that even though they share a deep love for Nicky and experience a common trauma, each of them is highly individualistic, the sisters have distinct personalities, careers, and outlooks. We observe each of them managing their grief privately and in their own unique way. The characters feel alive and the dialogues feel believable. While there is attention to detail in the writing, it was a miss for me, something about it read as if it were a bit too matter-of-fact. It could have been a little less "tell", and a bit more "show".

<a href="https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/195430687-blue-sisters?">Blue Sisters</a> is my first book from the author, and it left a lasting impression. <a href="https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/21373404.Coco_Mellors">Mellors</a> presents an unflinching exploration of complex family dynamics, grief, and addiction. I read this as someone who didn't grow up with sisters but has always wondered what it would be like to have grown up surrounded by women. In that regard, I have a soft spot for <a href="https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/195430687-blue-sisters?">Blue Sisters</a>, because much like <a href="https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/1934.Little_Women">Little Women</a> and <a href="https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/8127.Anne_of_Green_Gables?">Anne of Green Gables</a>, it's a compassionate and discerning exploration of sisterhood.
